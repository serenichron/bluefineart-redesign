# Virtual gallery: style brief for the first room (2026-11-10)

Phase 1 of the 3D gallery experiment. This brief says what the room should look like and how big it is.
Phase 2 builds it, empty. Phase 3 hangs the eleven Isaac Maimon works.

## What I looked at
- Nine Blue Gallery photos: the five homepage slides and four older ones from bluefineart.com/our-galleries.
- Kasmin Gallery, New York, and Xavier Hufkens, Brussels. Both through ERCO case studies, so the notes lean towards light.
- All room sizes below are my estimates from photos. Nobody measured anything.

## How Blue Gallery looks today
- **Walls:** pure white. Freestanding partition walls break the space into bays. They sit on a recessed base, so they seem to float.
- **Floor:** light, warm oak planks with a matte finish.
- **Ceiling:** open and painted white. Trusses, ducts and many small track spots are on show.
- **Light:** warm spots that make a pool on each work. Daylight comes in through a full glass front.
- **Sculpture:** on clear acrylic plinths. This is the most recognisable detail in the gallery.
- **Furniture:** grey armchairs, a grey sofa, a glass table and a rug. It reads as a living room, which fits "see it on your wall".
- **Colour:** the art carries it. The only brand colour is the blue of the sign and awnings (#00aeef).
- **Feel:** lively and full. Many works share each sightline.

## What the other two do differently
- **Kasmin:** one calm, column-free room. Walls are 23 ft high and the floor is polished concrete. A grid of concrete skylights gives soft, even daylight. Works hang far apart.
- **Hufkens:** rooms from 3.1 m to 9.1 m high, linked by steps. Walls are repainted per show (deep green in the photos). Spots and wallwashers work together: wallwashers light the whole wall evenly, spots pick out each work.

## Our room: Blue Gallery on its calmest day
Keep what makes it Blue Gallery. Borrow the calm from Kasmin and the wall colour idea from Hufkens.

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Walls | Warm white, matte | Blue Gallery's own. Maimon's colour needs it. |
| Floor | Light oak planks, matte, soft reflection | Blue Gallery's own. Warmer than concrete. |
| Ceiling | White, with a grid of soft glowing panels | Kasmin's even daylight. Calmer than ducts, and lighter for a phone to draw. |
| Light | Even wash on every wall, plus one warm spot per work | The Hufkens mix. The spot pool is what says "gallery". |
| Partition | One floating wall in the middle | Blue Gallery's signature. It hides part of the room, so there is something to walk around. |
| Seating | One grey bench or two armchairs on a rug | Gives scale and the living room feel. |
| Plinth | One clear acrylic plinth, empty for now | A place for sculpture later. |
| Accent | A thin line of brand blue at the doorway | The sign, quoted once. |
| Later | Wall colour can change per show | The room view on the exhibition page already has four wall colours. |

## Size
The eleven works are five squares about 1.5 m wide and six wide works about 2.0 m wide. None is taller than 1.5 m. Side by side they need 19.2 m of wall before any gaps.

- **Room:** 14 m long, 9 m wide, 4.5 m high.
- **Long walls:** three works each, with about 2 m between them.
- **Far wall:** two works.
- **Entry wall:** a 2 m doorway in the middle, one square work on each side.
- **Partition:** 4 m wide, 3.2 m high. It started at 5 m, which left too little room to stand back from the side walls. One work on the side facing the door. It is the first thing a visitor sees.
- **Hanging height:** the centre of every work at 1.47 m (58 in). The room view already uses this.
- **Eye height for the visitor:** 1.6 m.

That makes 3 + 3 + 2 + 2 + 1 = 11 works.

## Update, 2026-11-10: the room as built
Alex chose "their real gallery, plus staging". So the room now follows Blue Gallery's own space more closely than the brief above, and the calm skylight ceiling is gone.

**Taken from the real gallery**
- A glass front, 6 m wide, with double doors. Atlantic Avenue at dusk is painted beyond it: shopfronts, palms, street lamps, passing cars.
- The blue awning, seen from below through the top of the glass.
- An open white ceiling with bar joists, an air duct and black track on rods.
- A lounge behind the floating wall: grey sofa, two boucle armchairs, a glass table, a pale rug.
- Two clear acrylic plinths by the glass. The pieces on them are stand-ins (a chrome pebble and a red knot), not copies of any artist's work.

**The hang changed to make room for the glass**
- "Colors Igniting" moved to the back of the floating wall.
- "Reflections In Bloom" moved to the solid pier beside the glass.

**Staging**
- Each work brightens as the visitor nears it. Standing at one work, the others rest.
- Sound is off until asked for. It is made in the browser from filtered noise: a room tone, a slow wind, and a soft ripple near the paintings of water. Nobody has listened to it yet.

**Still painted light**
All light is still painted on. Baked light from Blender remains the biggest step up in realism.

## Update 2, 2026-11-10: two rooms on one engine
The Blue Gallery copy did not work. Alex's notes: too much street, doors and windows too big, chairs wrong, floor dull. He asked for a room that is modern, playful and "art itself". That room is gone from the code.

**`gallery-first.html`: the first room**
Oak floor, glowing ceiling grid, one floating wall, a bench. No staging and no sound. It is kept as the starting point, so a new design can begin from it.

**`gallery.html`: "Arches and colour"**
- Two cross walls split the room into three bays. Each has one tall arch, 3.6 m wide and 3.9 m high.
- Each bay has its own deep colour: teal, then plum, then night blue. The inside of each arch is lined in gold.
- From the door, the two arches frame "The Heart Of The Land" on the far wall.
- A ribbon of light runs overhead. It swings from side to side, dips under each arch and turns over as it goes. It takes the colour of the painting the visitor is nearest.
- The floor is pale terrazzo, polished enough to hold a faint reflection of the walls and paintings.
- The way in is one small arched doorway with a soft glow. No street.
- One plain bench in the middle bay. No chairs and no sculpture.
- Works brighten as the visitor nears them. The sound switch stays, still unheard by anyone.

**The hang in "Arches and colour"**
- Far wall: The Heart Of The Land.
- First cross wall, facing the door: Colors Igniting, Reflections In Bloom.
- Second cross wall, facing the door: Mountains Kiss The Sky, Dreams Around The Trees.
- The six wide works: one on each side wall of each bay.

## Update 3, 2026-11-10: the quiet room
"Arches and colour" was too much: too many strong colours, and it looked like a cheap video game. It is gone from the code. The research behind its replacement is in `gallery-surfaces-research.md`.

**`gallery.html` is now the quiet room**
- Same plan as the first room: one floating wall, a bench, eleven works.
- Floor: pale grey polished concrete with fine joints and a faint reflection.
- Walls: flat warm white, with a thin dark shadow line at the floor and no skirting board.
- Colour, both muted: dusk blue (#73808c) on the far wall, and a pale blue-grey (#aeb7bd) on the floating wall. The floating wall was clay (#b9a39a) at first. Alex found it too brown, so it became a paler cousin of the far wall.
- Ceiling: deep white coffers, each with one glowing panel.
- Door: one modest frosted glass door.

**Real light**
`tools/bake_light.py` works the light out ahead of time. The ceiling panels are the only source. Every surface is cut into 25 cm patches, and light bounces between them until it settles. So the walls are brighter near the ceiling, the bench and the floating wall leave soft shadows, and each coloured wall tints what is near it. The script takes about half a minute.

**`gallery-first.html`** is unchanged and still the starting point for new designs.

**Sound**
The noise-based sound is removed. Alex wants real recordings: quiet classical music, soft chatter, and footsteps. Candidates found so far:
- Music: Satie, Gymnopédie No. 1, played by Robin Alciatore. Public domain via Musopen, on Wikimedia Commons. 3.53 MB. Downloaded with Alex's go-ahead and wired to the sound switch. Credits in `assets/audio/CREDITS.md`.
- Chatter: "museum 1" by Auroch_Media on Freesound (CC BY 4.0, 51 s), or the Städel Museum recording by cfork (CC BY 4.0, 1 min 31 s). Both need a credit line. Freesound needs a login to download.
- Not usable: "British Museum voices" by ERH. Its licence forbids commercial use.
- Footsteps: nothing confirmed yet.

## Update 4, 2026-11-10: text wall, door, ceiling
- The floating wall swapped sides. Its front, facing the door, now carries the exhibition text: artist, title, dates, the introduction, the quote, and one line for Blue Gallery. "The Heart Of The Land" hangs on its back.
- The door is a tall oak leaf, 3 m high, in a dark bronze frame, with a slim frosted glass window beside it. Wood is the only warm material in the room.
- The ceiling is one soft sheet of light seen through deep white fins. The fins run across the room, and their depth rises and falls in a slow wave. The coffers are gone. The light was recomputed for the glowing ceiling.
- Focus view: the eye now glides to the height of whatever the visitor walks up to, so paintings and cards are seen square-on.
- Double-click a painting you stand at to zoom into that spot. Double-click again to zoom out.
- Sound starts on, begins with the visitor's first click, and the page remembers an "off" choice.
